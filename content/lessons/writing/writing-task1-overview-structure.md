## What the task asks

In Academic Task 1 you describe a **visual**: a line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table, process
diagram or map. Sometimes there are two visuals. You must write **at least 150 words in about 20
minutes**. The instruction always says something like:

> Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons
> where relevant.

Three key ideas are in that sentence: **summarise**, **select**, **compare**. You do not need to
describe every number. You need to choose the important ones and show how they relate.

## The four-paragraph structure

| Paragraph | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | Paraphrase what the visual shows | 1 sentence |
| 2. Overview | The two or three main features | 2 sentences |
| 3. Details A | Key data for one group of features | 3–4 sentences |
| 4. Details B | Key data for another group | 3–4 sentences |

There is **no conclusion** with an opinion. The overview does the job of a conclusion; you can
place it after the introduction or at the end, but after the introduction is safer and clearer.

## Writing the introduction

Paraphrase the task. Change the words and the structure, but keep the meaning.

> Task: *The graph below shows the number of international students enrolled at three
> universities between 2005 and 2020.*

> Introduction: *The line graph compares how many overseas students studied at three
> universities over a fifteen-year period from 2005.*

Useful changes: *shows → compares / illustrates*; *the number of → how many*; *international
→ overseas*; *between 2005 and 2020 → over a fifteen-year period*.

## Writing the overview

The overview answers the question: **"If I had to describe this visual in two sentences, what
would I say?"** Look for:

- the **overall trend** (up, down, stable)
- the **biggest or smallest** category
- any **major change** or crossover
- for processes: the number of stages, the start and the end
- for maps: the most significant changes

Do not include specific numbers in the overview. Begin with *Overall,* or *In general,*.

> *Overall, enrolments rose at all three universities, but the increase was far more dramatic at
> Northfield, which overtook its rivals to become the most popular choice by the end of the
> period.*

## Grouping the details

Before writing, decide how to divide the data into two body paragraphs. Common ways:

- **By trend:** the categories that rose / the categories that fell
- **By time:** the first half of the period / the second half
- **By category:** the two largest groups / the two smallest
- **By visual:** the bar chart / the pie chart

Each body paragraph should **compare**, not just list. "Northfield had 2,000 students in 2005"
is a fact. "Northfield began with 2,000 students, half the number at Easton" is a comparison.

## Worked example

> *The line graph compares how many overseas students studied at three universities over a
> fifteen-year period from 2005.*
>
> *Overall, enrolments rose at all three universities, but the increase was far more dramatic
> at Northfield, which overtook its rivals to become the most popular choice by the end of the
> period.*
>
> *In 2005, Easton had the largest international intake, at around 4,000 students, while
> Northfield and Westbury each had roughly half that figure. Easton's numbers grew only slowly,
> reaching about 5,000 by 2020.*
>
> *Northfield, by contrast, saw a steep rise after 2010, its student numbers tripling to
> approximately 6,500 in 2020. Westbury's growth was modest and steady, ending the period at just
> under 3,000, the lowest of the three.*

This report is about 135 words. In the exam you would add one more comparison, for example the
gap between Northfield and Easton in 2020, to pass the 150-word minimum safely.

## Common traps

- **No overview**, or an overview hidden in the middle of details.
- **Describing every number** in order, with no grouping or comparison.
- **Explaining causes** ("because the university became famous"). The visual does not tell you
  this.
- **Copying the task sentence** word for word. Copied words are not counted.
- **Wrong data.** Accuracy is part of Task Achievement. Check units: percentages, thousands,
  tonnes.

## Practice idea

Take any chart on BandUp. Before writing anything, spend three minutes answering on paper:
(1) What is the overall trend? (2) What is the biggest/smallest? (3) How will I group the data
into two paragraphs? Then write only the introduction and overview in five minutes. Practising
these two paragraphs separately builds the skill that most strongly affects your Task 1 band.
