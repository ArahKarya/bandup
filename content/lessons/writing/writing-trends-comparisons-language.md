## Why precise language matters

In Task 1, Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range together are half of your score. Many
candidates repeat "increase" and "decrease" again and again. Band 7 needs **range and
precision**: the right word for the size and speed of the change, used in the correct structure.

## Verbs of change

| Direction | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Up | rise, increase, grow, climb, go up |
| Up a lot | surge, soar, rocket, jump |
| Down | fall, decrease, decline, drop, go down |
| Down a lot | plummet, plunge, collapse |
| No change | remain stable, stay constant, level off, plateau |
| Up and down | fluctuate, vary |
| Highest point | peak at, reach a peak of |
| Lowest point | hit a low of, bottom out at |
| Recovery | recover, pick up, bounce back |

## Two structures: verb + adverb, or adjective + noun

You can describe the same change in two ways. Using both gives grammatical range.

- *Sales **increased sharply** in 2015.* (verb + adverb)
- *There was a **sharp increase** in sales in 2015.* (adjective + noun)

| Adjective | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| dramatic | dramatically | very large |
| sharp / steep | sharply / steeply | large and fast |
| significant / considerable | significantly / considerably | large |
| moderate | moderately | medium |
| steady | steadily | regular |
| gradual | gradually | slow, over time |
| slight / marginal | slightly / marginally | very small |

## Prepositions with numbers

These small words cause many errors:

- rose **from** 20% **to** 35% (start and end points)
- rose **by** 15 percentage points (the size of the change)
- **an increase of** 15% (amount) / **an increase in** sales (thing)
- peaked **at** 35% / **stood at** 20% in 2010
- **between** 2010 **and** 2020 / **over** the period / **by** 2020 (no later than)

Note: when a figure changes from 20% to 35%, it rises by **15 percentage points**, not by 15%.

## Comparing figures

**Comparatives and superlatives**

- *Rail was **more popular than** buses.*
- *Cycling was **the least common** method.*

**Multiples and fractions**

- *Car use was **twice as high as** rail use.*
- *Nearly **three times as many** men as women worked in construction.*
- *Spending on food **doubled** / **tripled** / **halved**.*
- *Just **under a quarter** of respondents… / **roughly a third**…*

**Contrast linkers**

- *While / Whereas exports rose, imports fell.*
- *Exports rose. **In contrast**, imports fell.*
- *Exports rose, **compared with** a fall in imports.*

**Avoiding repetition**

- *The figure for France was higher than **that of** Spain.*
- *Car ownership rose in both cities, but **the latter** saw a faster increase.*

## Worked example: improving a paragraph

Weak version:

> *In 2000 the price of coffee was 2 dollars. In 2005 it increased to 4 dollars. In 2010 it
> increased to 5 dollars. Tea price was 1 dollar and it increased a little.*

Improved version:

> *The price of coffee **doubled** in the first five years, **climbing from** $2 **to** $4,
> before **rising more gradually** to $5 by 2010. Tea, **by contrast**, remained much cheaper,
> **experiencing only a marginal increase** from its starting price of $1.*

Notice: varied verbs (doubled, climbing, rising), an adverb of speed (gradually), a contrast
linker, a noun phrase (a marginal increase) and a comparison (much cheaper).

## Common traps

- **Using "increase" for everything.** Choose a verb that shows size and speed.
- **Wrong prepositions:** "increased of 20%" should be "increased by 20%".
- **Over-dramatic words:** a rise from 30% to 32% is not a "surge". Accuracy beats impressive
  vocabulary.
- **Mixing tenses.** Past data uses the past simple; predictions use *is expected to / is
  predicted to*.
- **Writing "the number of" with uncountable nouns.** Use *the amount of* water, *the number of*
  cars.

## Practice idea

Choose five numbers that changed over time in your life (your height, your phone bill, hours of
sleep). For each, write two sentences: one with **verb + adverb** and one with **adjective +
noun**. Then write three comparison sentences using "twice as… as", "whereas" and "that of".
