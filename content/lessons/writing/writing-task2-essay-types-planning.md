## What the task asks

Task 2 is an essay of at least **250 words**. You should spend about **40 minutes** on it, and it
counts for about **twice as much** as Task 1. The topic is general (education, technology,
environment, work, society), and you do not need specialist knowledge. What you do need is to
**answer the exact question** with a clear position and well-developed ideas.

## The five main question types

| Type | Typical wording | What you must do |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion | To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Give and support a clear position |
| Discussion | Discuss both views and give your own opinion. | Explain both sides, then state your view |
| Advantages / disadvantages | Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? | Weigh both and decide which is greater |
| Problem / solution (or cause / solution) | What problems does this cause? How can they be solved? | Explain problems or causes and suggest solutions |
| Two-part (direct questions) | Why is this the case? Is this a positive or negative development? | Answer each question directly |

Some advantages/disadvantages questions simply say "Discuss the advantages and disadvantages",
with no request for an opinion. Read carefully: the wording tells you what is required.

## Step 1: Analyse the question (1 minute)

Underline:
- the **topic** (e.g. working from home)
- the **specific focus** (e.g. its effect on *young employees*)
- the **instruction** (e.g. *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?*)

Many essays lose marks because they write about the general topic but ignore the specific
focus.

## Step 2: Decide your position and ideas (3 minutes)

Write a one-sentence answer to the question. This is your thesis. Then brainstorm two main
ideas, each with a reason and an example.

## Step 3: Make a quick outline (1 minute)

A standard four-paragraph structure works for almost every type:

1. **Introduction:** paraphrase the topic + thesis (your answer)
2. **Body 1:** main idea 1 + explanation + example
3. **Body 2:** main idea 2 + explanation + example
4. **Conclusion:** restate your answer briefly

For a discussion essay, body 1 is the first view and body 2 is the second view (often the one
you agree with). For a problem/solution essay, body 1 covers problems and body 2 solutions.

## Worked example

> *In many countries, young people are increasingly choosing to work from home. Do the
> advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?*

**Analysis:** topic = working from home; focus = **young people**; instruction = weigh advantages
against disadvantages and decide.

**Thesis:** *Although remote work offers young employees flexibility, I believe the
disadvantages, particularly the loss of informal learning, are more significant at the start of
a career.*

**Outline:**
- Body 1 (advantage): saves commuting time and costs → more time for study or rest. Example:
  a graduate saving two hours a day.
- Body 2 (disadvantage, stronger): fewer chances to learn by watching experienced colleagues,
  weaker professional networks. Example: a junior accountant who never sees how senior staff
  handle difficult clients.
- Conclusion: disadvantages outweigh advantages for young workers; hybrid models may help.

**Introduction written from the plan:**

> *Remote working has become a common choice for many people who are just beginning their
> careers. While this arrangement offers clear practical benefits, I would argue that, for young
> employees in particular, its drawbacks are more serious.*

## Developing a body paragraph

Use the **PEEL** pattern:

- **P**oint: the topic sentence.
- **E**xplain: why or how this is true.
- **E**xample: a specific, realistic case.
- **L**ink: connect back to the question.

Band 7 Task Response needs ideas that are *extended and supported*. One well-explained idea is
better than three listed ideas.

## Common traps

- **Answering only part of the question,** for example giving causes but no solutions.
- **An unclear or changing position.** If you agree in the introduction and disagree in the
  conclusion, Task Response falls.
- **Memorised introductions** that do not mention the specific topic.
- **Writing about the general topic** and forgetting the focus (young people, rural areas,
  developing countries).
- **No planning,** leading to a confused second half.

## Practice idea

Collect ten Task 2 questions from BandUp. For each one, do only Steps 1–3 in five minutes: write
the type, the focus, your thesis and a four-line outline. Do not write the essay. After ten
plans, choose your two best and write them in full. This builds the planning habit quickly.
