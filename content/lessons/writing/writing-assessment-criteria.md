## Four criteria, equal weight

Every Writing task is marked against four criteria. Each is worth **25%** of the task band.

1. **Task Achievement** (Task 1) / **Task Response** (Task 2)
2. **Coherence and Cohesion**
3. **Lexical Resource**
4. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy**

The examiner gives a band for each criterion, and the task band is their average. Task 2 counts
for about twice as much as Task 1 in your Writing score. Understanding the criteria is the most
useful thing you can do for Writing, because it tells you **what to improve**.

## 1. Task Achievement / Task Response

This criterion asks: *Did you do what the task asked?*

**Task 1 (Academic):** Did you give a clear **overview** of the main trends or features? Did you
select and report the key details accurately? Did you make comparisons? Did you avoid giving
opinions?

**Task 1 (General Training):** Did you cover all three bullet points? Is the purpose of the
letter clear? Is the tone right for the reader?

**Task 2:** Did you answer **all parts** of the question? Is your **position** clear throughout?
Are your ideas extended and supported with explanations or examples?

| Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|
| Addresses all parts, but some more fully than others | Addresses all parts of the task |
| Position is relevant, but conclusions may be unclear or repetitive | Clear position throughout |
| Main ideas are relevant, but some are not well developed | Main ideas are extended and supported, though may over-generalise |

## 2. Coherence and Cohesion

**Coherence** is about logical organisation: does your essay flow from idea to idea? **Cohesion**
is about the tools that connect ideas: linking words, pronouns, referencing ("this problem",
"such measures") and paragraphing.

| Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|
| Arranges information coherently with overall progression | Logically organises information with clear progression |
| Uses cohesive devices effectively, but cohesion may be faulty or mechanical | Uses a range of cohesive devices appropriately, with some under- or over-use |
| Paragraphing may not always be logical | Presents a clear central topic within each paragraph |

The key message: **more linking words do not mean a higher band**. Good writers use them only
when needed and rely on referencing and logical order too.

## 3. Lexical Resource

This criterion measures your vocabulary: its **range**, its **accuracy**, and your awareness of
**collocation** (which words naturally go together) and **style**.

| Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|
| Adequate range for the task | Sufficient range for some flexibility and precision |
| Attempts less common vocabulary, with some inaccuracy | Uses less common items, with awareness of style and collocation |
| Some errors in spelling or word formation, but meaning is clear | Occasional errors in word choice, spelling or word formation |

Example: "Governments should *make* stricter laws" is fine. "Governments should *introduce*
stricter *legislation*" shows better collocation. But do not use rare words you are unsure of;
an incorrect word costs more than a simple correct one.

## 4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

This criterion looks at **range** (do you use different structures?) and **accuracy** (how many
of your sentences are error-free?).

| Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|
| Mix of simple and complex sentence forms | Variety of complex structures |
| Some errors, but they rarely reduce communication | Frequent error-free sentences |
| | Good control of grammar and punctuation, with a few errors |

Complex structures include relative clauses, conditionals, passive forms, noun clauses and
participle phrases. See the grammar lessons for practice.

## Worked example: diagnosing a paragraph

> "Nowadays, technology is very important. Many people use technology. Firstly, technology makes
> life easy. Secondly, technology helps students. Moreover, technology is good for business.
> In conclusion, technology has many advantages."

- **Task Response:** ideas are listed but never explained or supported. Band 5–6.
- **Coherence and Cohesion:** mechanical linking ("Firstly, Secondly, Moreover").
- **Lexical Resource:** "technology" repeated six times; "very important", "good" are basic.
- **Grammar:** only simple sentences.

An improved version:

> "Digital tools have transformed the way students learn. Online platforms, for example, allow
> learners in remote areas to access lectures that were once available only on university
> campuses, which narrows the gap between rural and urban education."

One idea, explained, with an example, precise vocabulary and a complex sentence.

## Common traps

- **Thinking grammar is everything.** Task Response is a quarter of your score; an off-topic
  essay cannot reach band 7.
- **Memorised phrases.** Examiners recognise them, and they do not show real ability.
- **Writing too little.** Under-length answers lose marks in Task Achievement/Response.

## Practice idea

Take an essay you have written. Read it four times, each time focusing on only one criterion.
Mark it with four colours: missing development (TR), weak links (CC), repeated words (LR) and
errors (GRA). The colour that appears most is your priority for the next two weeks.
