## Two non-data visuals

Not every Academic Task 1 has numbers. Two common types are:

- **Process diagrams:** a series of stages showing how something is made (bricks, chocolate) or
  how something happens in nature (the life cycle of an insect, the water cycle).
- **Maps:** usually two or three maps of the same place at different times, showing how it has
  changed or will change.

The four-paragraph structure from the Task 1 overview lesson still works: introduction,
overview, two body paragraphs.

## Part 1: Processes

### Natural or man-made?

- A **natural process** (a life cycle) is usually described in the **active**: *The female
  lays her eggs on a leaf.*
- A **man-made process** (manufacturing) is usually described in the **passive**: *The clay is
  dug from the ground.* The passive is natural here because the action is important, not the
  worker.

### Overview

State how many stages there are, the first stage and the final product or result, and whether
the process is linear or a cycle.

> *Overall, there are seven stages in the production of glass bottles from recycled glass,
> beginning with collection and ending with the delivery of new bottles to shops.*

### Sequencing language

- **First / Firstly / To begin with / The process starts when…**
- **Next / Then / After that / Subsequently / Following this…**
- **Once / After / As soon as** + clause: *Once the clay has been shaped, it is dried.*
- **Before / Prior to** + noun or -ing: *Before being packed, the bricks are cooled.*
- **Finally / In the final stage / At the end of the process…**

Vary the linkers. A report that starts every sentence with "Then" is mechanical.

### Worked example (process)

> *To begin with, used glass is collected from recycling points and transported to a
> processing plant, where it is sorted by colour. It is then washed to remove any dirt and
> crushed into small pieces. Once crushed, the glass is mixed with sand and heated to around
> 1,500°C until it melts. This liquid glass is subsequently poured into moulds to form new
> bottles, which are finally delivered to shops and supermarkets.*

Notice: the passive throughout, a range of linkers, a participle clause ("Once crushed") and a
relative clause ("which are finally delivered").

## Part 2: Maps

### Overview

Describe the most significant overall changes. Did the area become more developed? More
residential? Did nature disappear? Did transport improve?

> *Overall, the village was transformed from a farming community into a residential suburb,
> with most farmland replaced by housing and new road connections.*

### Language of location

- *in the north / to the east of / in the south-west corner*
- *next to / opposite / alongside the river / along the main road*
- *in the centre of the town / on the outskirts / at the edge of*

### Language of change

| Change | Language |
|---|---|
| Built | was constructed, was built, was erected, a new… was added |
| Removed | was demolished, was knocked down, was cleared, made way for |
| Changed | was converted into, was replaced by, was transformed into |
| Bigger/smaller | was extended, was expanded, was enlarged / was reduced in size |
| Roads | was widened, was extended, a road was built linking X and Y |
| Nature | trees were cut down, the forest was cleared, a park was created |

### Worked example (map)

> *In 1995, the area to the north of the river was covered by farmland, but by 2020 these
> fields had been cleared to make way for a large housing estate. The old factory beside the
> bridge was converted into a shopping centre, and a car park was built opposite it. The main
> road, meanwhile, was widened and extended eastwards to connect with the new motorway.*

Notice: past simple passives, a past perfect passive ("had been cleared") to show the change was
complete by 2020, location phrases and the linker "meanwhile".

## Common traps

- **Missing stages.** In a process, every stage should be mentioned, briefly.
- **Copying labels as a list.** Turn labels into full sentences: "crushing" becomes "the glass
  is crushed".
- **Explaining why** a change happened on a map. You do not know why; describe what changed.
- **Confusing tense.** For future maps ("planned development"), use *will be built / is
  planned to be*.
- **No overview.** It is as important here as in data reports.

## Practice idea

Think of something you do every week, for example making a cup of tea or washing clothes. Write
an eight-sentence description in the passive using six different sequencers. Then draw a simple
map of your neighbourhood now and ten years ago, and write five sentences about what was built,
removed or converted.
