## What the task asks

In General Training Writing Task 1 you write a **letter** of at least **150 words** in about
**20 minutes**. You are given a situation and **three bullet points** that you must cover. For
example:

> You recently stayed at a hotel and left an item in your room. Write a letter to the hotel
> manager. In your letter:
> - describe the item you left
> - explain where you think you left it
> - say how you would like it to be returned to you

The letter can be **formal**, **semi-formal** or **informal**. Choosing the right tone is part of
Task Achievement.

## Deciding the tone

Ask: **Who is the reader, and what is my relationship with them?**

| Type | Reader | Opening | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal | A manager, company or official you do not know | Dear Sir or Madam, | Yours faithfully, |
| Formal (named) | An official whose name you know | Dear Mr Lee, / Dear Ms Harper, | Yours sincerely, |
| Semi-formal | A neighbour, a teacher, a landlord you know | Dear Mr Lee, / Dear Anna, | Kind regards, / Best wishes, |
| Informal | A friend or family member | Dear Tom, / Hi Tom, | Best wishes, / See you soon, / Take care, |

## Features of each register

**Formal**
- No contractions: *I am writing* not *I'm writing*.
- Polite, indirect requests: *I would be grateful if you could…*
- Formal vocabulary: *receive, inform, require, unfortunately, at your earliest convenience*.

**Semi-formal**
- Polite but friendly. Contractions are possible but not overused.
- *I was wondering if you could…*, *I'd really appreciate it if…*

**Informal**
- Contractions, phrasal verbs, questions and exclamations.
- *It was great to hear from you!*, *Can you believe it?*, *Let me know what you think.*

Mixing registers is a common problem: *Dear Sir or Madam, I'm gonna tell you about my bag* is
both wrong in tone and in style.

## The structure

1. **Opening** line (Dear…)
2. **Purpose paragraph:** one or two sentences explaining why you are writing.
3. **One paragraph per bullet point.** Develop each one with details.
4. **Closing paragraph:** what you want to happen next, or a friendly sign-off.
5. **Closing phrase and your name.** (For formal letters, write your full name.)

## Worked example (formal)

> *Dear Sir or Madam,*
>
> *I am writing regarding a personal item that I believe I left at your hotel during my stay
> from 12 to 15 March.*
>
> *The item is a small black leather wallet containing my driving licence, two bank cards and a
> family photograph. Although it has little financial value, the photograph is of great
> sentimental importance to me.*
>
> *I stayed in Room 314, and I am fairly certain that I left the wallet in the drawer of the
> bedside table, as I remember placing it there on my final evening. It is also possible that it
> fell behind the desk while I was packing.*
>
> *If the wallet is found, I would be grateful if you could send it to my home address by
> registered post. I would, of course, be happy to cover any postage costs. Alternatively, I could
> collect it in person next weekend.*
>
> *Thank you in advance for your help. I look forward to hearing from you.*
>
> *Yours faithfully,*
>
> *Daniel Hartono*

Each bullet has its own paragraph with **specific details**: colour, contents, room number, the
reason for the request.

## Useful phrases

- **Purpose (formal):** *I am writing to complain about / enquire about / request…*
- **Purpose (informal):** *Just a quick note to tell you… / I'm writing to say thanks for…*
- **Apologising:** *I apologise for any inconvenience. / Sorry I haven't been in touch.*
- **Requesting:** *Could you possibly…? / I would appreciate it if…*
- **Closing (formal):** *I look forward to your reply.*
- **Closing (informal):** *Can't wait to see you. / Write back soon!*

## Common traps

- **Missing or under-developing a bullet point.** Each one needs at least two sentences.
- **Wrong opening and closing pair.** "Dear Sir or Madam" with "Yours sincerely" is a classic
  error.
- **Starting without a purpose.** The reader should know why you are writing in the first two
  lines.
- **Memorised phrases that do not fit the situation.**
- **Writing far too much.** 160–200 words is enough. Use the time to check accuracy.

## Practice idea

Take one situation, such as inviting someone to a celebration, and write **three short
versions**: one to a close friend, one to your neighbour, and one to your company's director.
Keep the content the same and change only the tone. Comparing the three side by side is the
fastest way to master register.
