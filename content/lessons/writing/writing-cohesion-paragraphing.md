## What examiners look for

Coherence and Cohesion is 25% of your Writing band. Band 7 requires that you:

- **logically organise** information and ideas, with **clear progression** throughout;
- use a **range of cohesive devices** appropriately (though there may be some under- or
  over-use);
- present a **clear central topic within each paragraph**.

Many learners think cohesion means linking words. Linking words are one tool, but strong
writers also use **referencing**, **substitution**, **lexical chains** and clear paragraph
structure.

## Paragraphing: one idea per paragraph

Each body paragraph should have one **central topic**, introduced in a **topic sentence**. Every
other sentence should support that idea. If you start a new idea, start a new paragraph.

A simple model:

1. **Topic sentence:** the main point.
2. **Explanation:** why or how.
3. **Example or evidence.**
4. **Result or link** back to the question.

Avoid one-sentence paragraphs and very long paragraphs that mix several ideas.

## Cohesive tools

### 1. Linkers (use them with care)

| Function | Examples |
|---|---|
| Addition | in addition, furthermore, also, not only… but also |
| Contrast | however, although, whereas, on the other hand, nevertheless |
| Cause / result | because, since, as a result, therefore, consequently, this leads to |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as |
| Concession | admittedly, while it is true that, despite |
| Conclusion | in conclusion, overall, to sum up |

Use them only where the relationship needs to be made clear. One or two per paragraph is often
enough.

### 2. Referencing

Words like *this, that, these, it, they, such, the former, the latter* point back to earlier
ideas.

> *Governments could tax sugary drinks. **This measure** would raise money for health services.*

"This measure" is stronger than "This" alone, because it summarises the idea.

### 3. Substitution and ellipsis

> *Some students prefer online courses, while **others** prefer face-to-face **ones**.*

"Others" replaces "other students"; "ones" replaces "courses".

### 4. Lexical chains

Use related words and synonyms across a paragraph: *pollution → emissions → contaminated air →
this environmental damage*. This keeps the topic clear without repetition.

## Worked example: before and after

**Before:**

> *Technology is used in schools. Moreover, technology helps students. Furthermore, students can
> find information. Moreover, teachers use technology. In addition, technology is expensive.*

Problems: repeated "technology", mechanical linkers, ideas jump from benefits to cost with no
clear progression, no central topic.

**After:**

> *Digital technology has become central to modern classrooms, largely because it gives students
> instant access to information. Rather than relying on a single textbook, learners can compare
> several sources and evaluate them, a skill that is increasingly valued by employers. Teachers
> also benefit, as these tools allow them to track each pupil's progress and adapt lessons
> accordingly. The main barrier, however, is cost, which means that such advantages are often
> limited to wealthier schools.*

Notice: a topic sentence, referencing ("it", "them", "these tools", "such advantages"), a
lexical chain (students → learners → pupils), only two explicit linkers ("also", "however"),
and a logical move from benefits to a limitation.

## Common traps

- **Overusing linkers,** especially "Moreover" and "Furthermore".
- **Wrong linkers:** "On the other hand" needs a real contrast with the previous idea, not just a
  new point.
- **Unclear reference:** "This is a problem" when "this" could mean three different things.
  Add a noun: "This shortage is a problem."
- **"Besides" and "Despite" errors:** *Despite of* is wrong; use *despite + noun* or *although +
  clause*.
- **No paragraphs,** or paragraphs that are separated only by a new line in the middle of an
  idea.

## Practice idea

Take an essay you wrote recently. Highlight every linking word. If there are more than two per
paragraph, remove some and replace their job with referencing ("this approach", "such
policies"). Then write the central topic of each paragraph in the margin in five words. If you
cannot do it, the paragraph needs reorganising.
