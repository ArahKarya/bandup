## What happens in Part 2

The examiner gives you a **task card** with a topic and three or four bullet points, plus a
pencil and paper. You have **one minute** to prepare, then you speak for **one to two minutes**.
The examiner will stop you at two minutes. A typical card:

> **Describe a skill you learned that you are proud of.**
> You should say:
> - what the skill is
> - when and how you learned it
> - what difficulties you had
> and explain why you are proud of it.

You do not have to cover the bullets in order, but they give you a ready-made structure.

## Using the one minute

Do **not** write sentences. You will not have time, and reading aloud sounds unnatural. Instead,
write **three to five keywords per bullet**. Many candidates divide the paper into four boxes:

| What | When / how |
|---|---|
| swimming, age 25, can't swim as kid | YouTube + local pool, 6 months, Saturday mornings |
| **Difficulties** | **Why proud** |
| fear of deep water, breathing, embarrassed beside kids | overcame fear, now swim with nephew, confidence |

Spend the most time on the **final "explain" point**. It is where you can show reasons, feelings
and more complex grammar.

## A structure for two minutes

1. **Opening (10 seconds):** introduce the topic. *I'd like to talk about…*
2. **Bullets 1–3 (about 75 seconds):** 20–25 seconds each, with details and a small story.
3. **Explanation (about 30 seconds):** the reason, your feelings, what it means to you now.
4. **If time remains:** add a comparison or a future plan.

## Worked example

> *I'd like to talk about learning to swim, which I only did quite recently, when I was about
> twenty-five. It might sound a bit strange, but I grew up far from the coast and nobody in my
> family could swim, so I never had the chance as a kid.*
>
> *I learned mostly by myself, to be honest. I watched a lot of beginner videos online and then
> went to a public pool near my office every Saturday morning. It took around six months before I
> could swim a full length without stopping.*
>
> *The hardest part was definitely the fear. Whenever I went near the deep end, I'd panic and
> forget how to breathe properly. It was also quite embarrassing, because I was often the only
> adult in the beginners' area, surrounded by confident eight-year-olds.*
>
> *The reason I'm proud of it is that it wasn't really about swimming. It was about overcoming
> something I'd been scared of my whole life. Last month I took my nephew to the beach and we
> swam together, which is something I simply couldn't have imagined a few years ago. If I'm
> honest, it's made me a bit braver in other areas too, like speaking up at work.*

This answer lasts about two minutes. It uses past tenses, a past habit ("I'd panic"), a third
conditional-style idea ("couldn't have imagined"), and natural phrases ("to be honest", "if I'm
honest").

## Keeping going

If you run out of ideas early, use one of these "extenders":

- **Describe more:** what did it look like, sound like, feel like?
- **Add a person:** who was with you, and what did they say?
- **Compare:** *Compared with…, it was much…*
- **Look forward:** *In the future, I'd like to…*

## Useful phrases

- *I'd like to talk about… / The… I'm going to describe is…*
- *What happened was… / It all started when…*
- *The tricky part was… / What I found most challenging was…*
- *Looking back, I think… / What made it special was…*

## Common traps

- **Writing full sentences** in the preparation minute.
- **Speaking for under a minute.** The examiner may wait silently; that wastes your chance.
- **Choosing a "perfect" topic** and spending 30 seconds deciding. Pick the first reasonable idea.
  You can invent details if you need to; this is a language test, not a truth test.
- **Telling a memorised story** that does not match the card.
- **Ignoring the "explain" point.**

## Practice idea

Use a timer. Take any BandUp Part 2 card, prepare for exactly one minute with keywords only,
then record yourself for two minutes. Do not stop the recording if you pause. Afterwards, write
down how long you spoke and which bullet you said least about. Do one card every day for two
weeks, and the two minutes will start to feel short.
