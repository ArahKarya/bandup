## The format

The Speaking test is a **face-to-face interview** with an examiner (sometimes by video call at a
test centre). It lasts **11–14 minutes** and is recorded. It is the same for Academic and General
Training.

| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction and interview | 4–5 minutes | Questions about familiar topics: home, work, study, hobbies, daily life |
| 2. Long turn | 3–4 minutes | You receive a task card, prepare for 1 minute, then speak for 1–2 minutes |
| 3. Discussion | 4–5 minutes | Abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic |

In Part 2 you can write notes on paper. The examiner will stop you after two minutes and may ask
one short follow-up question.

## The four criteria

Each is worth **25%**.

### 1. Fluency and Coherence

- Can you speak **at length** without many pauses or repetition?
- Do you use **linking words and discourse markers** naturally (*well, actually, the thing is,
  on top of that*)?
- Are your ideas **logically connected**?

Band 7 speakers speak at length without noticeable effort and use a range of connectives
flexibly. They may sometimes hesitate to find language, but not to find ideas.

### 2. Lexical Resource

- Do you have enough vocabulary to talk about many topics?
- Do you use some **less common words and idiomatic language**, with awareness of style and
  collocation?
- Can you **paraphrase** when you do not know a word?

Example: "I like cooking" is band 5–6. "I've really got into cooking lately; it's become a way of
unwinding after work" shows band 7 range.

### 3. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

- Do you use a **mix of simple and complex structures**?
- How many of your sentences are **error-free**?

Use conditionals ("If I had more time, I'd…"), relative clauses ("the friend who introduced me
to…"), different tenses ("I've been living there since…") and comparisons naturally.

### 4. Pronunciation

- Are you **easy to understand** throughout?
- Do you use features such as **word stress, sentence stress, intonation and chunking** to help
  meaning?

Accent is **not** penalised. An Indonesian accent is fine if your sounds, stress and rhythm are
clear. Common issues for Indonesian speakers include dropping final consonants ("work" sounding
like "wor"), the /θ/ and /ð/ sounds in "think" and "this", and a flat rhythm with equal stress on
every word.

## Worked example: same answer, different bands

**Question:** *Do you enjoy cooking?*

**Band 5 style:**
> "Yes. I like cooking. I cook rice and fried egg. It is easy."

Very short, simple vocabulary and grammar, no development.

**Band 7 style:**
> "Yes, I do, actually. I didn't use to cook much, but since I moved into my own flat I've had to
> learn, and now I really enjoy it. I mostly make simple Indonesian dishes like nasi goreng,
> although I've been experimenting with pasta recently. It's quite relaxing after a long day."

Longer, linked naturally ("actually", "but since", "although"), a range of tenses ("didn't use
to", "I've had to", "I've been experimenting"), precise vocabulary ("experimenting", "relaxing").

## How to behave in the test

- **Speak more than the examiner.** Your answers are what is being assessed.
- **Ask for repetition if needed:** "Sorry, could you repeat the question?" This is not
  penalised.
- **Do not memorise answers.** Examiners are trained to spot them, and they may change the topic.
- **Mistakes are normal.** If you notice one, correct it quickly and carry on.
- **The examiner's neutral face is normal.** They are not allowed to react much.

## Common traps

- **One-word answers in Part 1.**
- **Stopping after 40 seconds in Part 2.**
- **Using "big words" incorrectly.** Accuracy and natural use beat difficult vocabulary.
- **Speaking too fast** to sound fluent, which makes pronunciation unclear.

## Practice idea

Record yourself answering four Part 1 questions. Listen back and give yourself a rough score
for each criterion. Write one improvement for each one, such as "add a reason", "use one past
tense", "stress the key word". Record the same answers again the next day and compare.
