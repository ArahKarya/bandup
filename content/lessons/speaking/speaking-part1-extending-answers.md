## What Part 1 is

Part 1 lasts **4–5 minutes**. The examiner asks questions on two or three familiar topics, such as
your home, work or studies, and then topics like weather, music, food, transport or weekends.
The questions are easy to understand. The challenge is that **short answers give the examiner
nothing to assess**. If you answer "Yes, I do" or "Maybe", you lose the chance to show fluency,
vocabulary and grammar.

But Part 1 is not a speech either. Aim for **two to four sentences**, about 15–25 seconds.

## The ARE method

A simple structure for almost any Part 1 question:

- **A – Answer** directly: *Yes, I do. / Not really. / It depends.*
- **R – Reason**: *because… / mainly since…*
- **E – Extend** with one of these:
  - an **example** (*For instance, last weekend I…*)
  - a **contrast** (*although… / but…*)
  - a **past/present comparison** (*When I was younger I…, but now…*)
  - a **future** idea (*I'd love to… one day*)
  - a **feeling** (*It makes me feel…*)

## Worked examples

**Q: Do you prefer mornings or evenings?**

> *Definitely evenings. (A) I'm not really a morning person, so I find it hard to concentrate
> before about ten. (R) When I was at school I had to get up at five, which was a struggle, but
> now that I work flexible hours I can get my most important tasks done after dinner. (E –
> past/present)*

**Q: How often do you use public transport?**

> *Almost every day, actually. (A) I take the TransJakarta bus to work because driving in rush
> hour is just exhausting. (R) The only downside is that it gets really packed, so if I'm not in
> a hurry I sometimes leave a bit later to avoid the crowds. (E – contrast)*

**Q: Is there a food you didn't like as a child but enjoy now?**

> *Yes, bitter melon. (A) As a kid I couldn't stand the taste, but my grandmother kept cooking it
> because she said it was healthy. (R) These days I actually order it in restaurants, so I guess
> my taste buds have changed. (E – past/present)*

## Useful phrases

**Starting:**
- *Yes, I do, actually.* / *Not really, to be honest.* / *It depends on…* / *I'd say…*

**Buying a moment:**
- *That's an interesting question; I've never really thought about it.*
- *Let me think… / Well…*

**Adding:**
- *On top of that… / Another thing is… / What I like most is…*

**Contrasting:**
- *Having said that… / Then again… / The only downside is…*

## Topic vocabulary

Prepare small vocabulary sets for common Part 1 topics. For "hometown": *bustling, laid-back,
the outskirts, a close-knit community, rapidly developing*. For "work": *a nine-to-five job,
deadlines, colleagues, flexible hours, career prospects*. Learn them in phrases, not alone.

## When you have no strong opinion

Some Part 1 questions seem boring or strange: *Do you like looking at the sky?* You do not need a
passionate answer. Say so honestly, then extend anyway:

> *I can't say I think about it much, to be honest. I suppose I notice it more when I'm on
> holiday, because in Jakarta the sky is often quite hazy, whereas in the countryside you can
> actually see the stars.*

An honest "not really" plus a reason and a contrast is a perfectly good band 7 answer.

## Common traps

- **Very short answers:** "Yes." "No." "Sometimes."
- **Over-long answers:** a two-minute monologue in Part 1 means the examiner has to interrupt.
- **Memorised scripts:** they sound unnatural, and the examiner may move on with a different
  question.
- **Repeating the question:** "Do I like rainy days? I like rainy days because…" sounds
  mechanical.
- **Answering a different question** because you prepared a story you want to use.

## Practice idea

Write down ten Part 1 questions on three topics. For each, record a 20-second answer using the
ARE method and a different extension technique each time. Listen back and count your sentences.
If any answer is one sentence, record it again with a reason and an extension.
