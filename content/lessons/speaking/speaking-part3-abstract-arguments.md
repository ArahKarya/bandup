## What Part 3 is

Part 3 lasts **4–5 minutes** and develops the topic of Part 2. If your Part 2 card was about a
skill you learned, Part 3 might ask:

- *What skills do young people need most today?*
- *Should schools teach practical skills like cooking and managing money?*
- *How might the skills employers want change in the future?*

These questions are **abstract**: they ask about people in general, society, causes, effects
and the future. They are the main place where the examiner tests whether you can reach band 7
and above. Answers should be longer than Part 1, around **30–60 seconds**.

## The PEEC structure

- **P – Position:** answer the question directly.
- **E – Explain:** give the reason.
- **E – Example:** support it with a specific case (from your country, the news or general
  knowledge).
- **C – Consider:** mention another view, an exception or a condition.

## Worked example

**Q: Should schools teach practical skills like cooking and managing money?**

> *(P) Yes, I think they definitely should, especially money management. (E) The main reason is
> that young people are now making financial decisions much earlier than their parents did. With
> online shopping and "buy now, pay later" apps, it's incredibly easy to get into debt before you
> even understand how interest works. (E) In Indonesia, for example, there have been quite a lot
> of news stories about university students struggling with online loans. (C) Having said that,
> I don't think it should replace academic subjects. Maybe it could be a short course in the
> final year, so schools don't lose time for maths or science.*

This answer gives a clear opinion, a developed reason, a real-world example and a balanced
condition. It also uses complex grammar: a comparative clause, a relative idea, "before you even
understand", a modal suggestion.

## Types of Part 3 question and useful language

| Question type | Example | Useful language |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion | Do you think…? | *I'd argue that… / In my view… / I'm convinced that…* |
| Comparison | How is X different now from the past? | *Compared with… / Whereas in the past… / These days…* |
| Cause | Why do people…? | *One reason is… / This is largely due to… / It stems from…* |
| Effect | What impact does X have? | *This can lead to… / As a result… / One consequence is…* |
| Future | How will X change? | *It's likely that… / I imagine… / We might see… / In all probability…* |
| Evaluation | Is this a good or bad thing? | *On the plus side… / The downside is… / On balance…* |

## When you do not know the answer

The examiner is testing your English, not your expertise. If a question is difficult:

> *That's not something I've thought about much, to be honest. But if I had to guess, I'd say
> that… because…*

Then reason logically. A thoughtful guess with good language scores well. "I don't know" scores
nothing.

## Making ideas more abstract

Move from personal examples to general patterns:

- *I* → *people of my generation / many young adults*
- *my city* → *large urban areas / developing economies*
- *my school* → *the education system*

Use **generalising language**: *tend to, generally speaking, by and large, in most cases*. Use
**hedging**: *it seems that, to some extent, this may be partly because*.

## Common traps

- **Answering only with personal stories.** Use them as examples, then generalise.
- **Very short answers.** Part 3 needs development: aim for at least four sentences.
- **Strong, unsupported claims:** "Everyone hates their job." Hedge and support.
- **Panicking when you disagree with the examiner's question.** You can disagree: *Actually, I'm
  not sure that's true, because…*
- **Ignoring the question word.** A "why" question needs causes, not a description.

## Practice idea

Choose one Part 2 topic and write six Part 3 questions about it, using the question types in the
table. Answer each one aloud with the PEEC structure, recording yourself. Check two things in
every answer: did you include a specific example, and did you consider another view? Practise
until both happen without thinking.
