## The real problem in IELTS Reading

Most candidates who lose marks in Reading do not lose them because the English is too hard.
They lose them because they **run out of time**. There are three passages, about 2,500–2,800
words in total in the Academic test, and 40 questions in 60 minutes. If you read every word
carefully, you will not finish. The solution is to use different reading speeds for different
jobs.

## Two essential skills

**Skimming** means reading quickly to understand the general idea. You look at:

- the title and any subtitle
- the first sentence of each paragraph (often the topic sentence)
- words that repeat across the passage
- the final paragraph

Skimming one passage should take about **two to three minutes**. After skimming, you should be
able to say in one sentence what each paragraph is about.

**Scanning** means searching for a specific piece of information without reading everything.
You move your eyes over the text looking for a target: a name, a date, a number, a technical
word with a capital letter. Scanning is fastest when the target is easy to see, so choose **key
words that cannot be paraphrased**, such as "1987", "Professor Lindqvist" or "Antarctica".

After scanning finds the right place, you switch to **careful reading** of the two or three
sentences around it. This is where you answer the question.

## A time plan for 60 minutes

| Passage | Suggested time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 17 minutes | Usually the easiest |
| 2 | 20 minutes | Medium difficulty |
| 3 | 23 minutes | Usually the hardest and longest |

On paper, you must also write your answers on the answer sheet inside these 60 minutes. Many
candidates write answers directly onto the answer sheet as they go to save time at the end.

## Step-by-step method for each passage

1. **Skim the passage** (2–3 minutes). Note the topic of each paragraph in two or three words
   in the margin.
2. **Read the first group of questions.** Underline key words, especially scannable ones.
3. **Scan** for the key word or a likely synonym.
4. **Read carefully** around the location and answer.
5. **Move to the next question.** In most question types, answers follow passage order, so the
   next answer is usually below the last one.
6. **Watch the clock.** If one question takes more than two minutes, guess and mark it to return.

## Worked example

Question: *In which year did the museum first allow visitors to touch the exhibits?*

Key words: "first", "touch", "exhibits". None of these is easy to scan, and "touch" could be
paraphrased ("hands-on", "handle"). But the question asks for a **year**, so scan for
**four-digit numbers**. You find 1952, 1978 and 1994. Now read around each:

> "When it opened in 1952, the museum kept everything behind glass. It was not until 1978 that
> a new director introduced hands-on displays, a move that was widely copied after 1994."

The answer is **1978**. "Hands-on displays" is the paraphrase of "allow visitors to touch".
1952 and 1994 are traps: they are near the key idea but answer different questions.

## Common traps

- **Reading the whole passage carefully before looking at the questions.** This wastes time.
  Skim first, read in detail only where questions send you.
- **Scanning for a word that is paraphrased.** If the question says "expensive", the passage may
  say "costly" or "at a high price". Scan for names and numbers where possible.
- **Spending too long on one question.** A single difficult question is worth the same one mark
  as an easy one.
- **Leaving blanks.** Always write something. A guess has a chance; a blank has none.

## Practice idea

Take any BandUp reading passage and set a timer for **three minutes**. Skim it and write a
four-word summary for each paragraph. Then check your summaries by reading the passage
carefully. Next, write five questions with numbers or names in them and time how quickly you can
scan for each one. Aim for under 30 seconds per item. Repeat with a new passage every few days.
