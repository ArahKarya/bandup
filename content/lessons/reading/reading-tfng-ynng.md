## The three answers

These questions give you statements and ask whether they agree with the passage.

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| TRUE / YES | The passage says the same thing (usually in different words). |
| FALSE / NO | The passage says the **opposite**, or something that cannot be true at the same time. |
| NOT GIVEN | The passage **does not say** whether it is true or false. |

**TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN** is used for factual information. **YES/NO/NOT GIVEN** is used for the
writer's opinions or claims ("Does the writer agree…?"). The method is the same.

## The key difference: FALSE vs NOT GIVEN

This is where most marks are lost. Ask yourself one question:

> **Can I point to words in the passage that make the statement impossible?**

- If yes, the answer is **FALSE**.
- If the passage is silent, or only talks about a related topic, the answer is **NOT GIVEN**.

Do not use your own knowledge. A statement might be true in real life but still be NOT GIVEN in
the passage.

## Words that change meaning

Watch these words closely, because one word can change the answer:

- **Quantity:** all, most, some, few, none, only
- **Frequency:** always, usually, sometimes, never
- **Certainty:** definitely, probably, may, might
- **Comparison:** more than, the first, the largest, the only
- **Time:** before, after, until, currently, used to

If the passage says "some scientists believe" and the statement says "scientists agree", that is
not the same.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Read the statement** and underline the key words and any "qualifier" words (most, only,
   first).
2. **Find the right place** in the passage by scanning for a name, number or topic word.
   Answers follow passage order.
3. **Read the sentences carefully**, comparing every part of the statement.
4. **Decide:** Same meaning → TRUE. Contradiction → FALSE. Not enough information → NOT GIVEN.
5. **Check the whole statement.** If one part is supported and another part is missing, the
   answer is usually NOT GIVEN. If one part is contradicted, it is FALSE.

## Worked example

Passage:

> "The city's first public library opened in 1854, funded entirely by donations from local
> merchants. Although it was free to enter, borrowing books required a small annual fee until
> 1901. Today it holds more than two million items, making it the second-largest collection in
> the region."

Statements:

1. *The library was paid for by the city government.* **FALSE**. It was "funded entirely by
   donations from local merchants", so it cannot have been paid for by the government.
2. *Before 1901, people had to pay to borrow books.* **TRUE**. "Borrowing books required a small
   annual fee until 1901."
3. *The library was very popular when it first opened.* **NOT GIVEN**. Nothing is said about
   visitor numbers or popularity in 1854.
4. *The library has the largest collection in the region.* **FALSE**. "The second-largest"
   means another collection is bigger, so this statement cannot be true. Be careful with a
   phrase like "one of the largest": it does not confirm "the largest", but it does not clearly
   contradict it either, so on its own it would usually lead to NOT GIVEN. Read superlatives
   very precisely.

## Yes/No/Not Given

With opinions, look for words that show the writer's attitude: *unfortunately, it is clear
that, I would argue, surprisingly, there is little evidence that*. If the writer says "There is
little evidence that the scheme reduced traffic", the statement "The writer believes the scheme
was successful in reducing traffic" is **NO**.

## Common traps

- **Using general knowledge** instead of the passage.
- **Choosing FALSE when the information is simply missing.**
- **Ignoring small words** such as "only" or "all".
- **Spending too long.** If you cannot find any related text after a careful search, the answer
  is probably NOT GIVEN. Note it and move on.

## Practice idea

Take one paragraph from any article. Write three statements about it: one TRUE, one FALSE and one
NOT GIVEN. For the FALSE one, change a qualifier ("some" to "all"). For the NOT GIVEN one, add a
reason or detail that the paragraph never mentions. Writing your own statements makes the logic
of the task very clear.
