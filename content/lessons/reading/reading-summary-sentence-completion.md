## Two versions of the task

**1. Words from the passage.** You fill gaps with words copied from the text. The instruction
gives a limit, for example *Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage*.

**2. Words from a box.** You choose from a list of words (A–J) given below the summary. These
words are often **synonyms**, not the exact words from the passage.

Sentence completion works like the first version, but each gap is in a separate sentence rather
than a paragraph.

## Why summaries help you

A summary usually covers **one part** of the passage (two or three paragraphs) and follows the
same order. The title of the summary and the first sentence tell you where to start reading.
Once you find the start, the remaining answers are usually close by.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Read the instruction** and circle the word limit and whether numbers are allowed.
2. **Read the whole summary** quickly to understand the topic.
3. **Find the matching part** of the passage. Scan for names, numbers or unusual words from the
   summary.
4. **For each gap, predict**:
   - the word type (noun, verb, adjective, number)
   - singular or plural
   - the meaning (a cause? a material? a place?)
5. **Read the passage sentence that matches the summary sentence.** Find the word that fills the
   gap.
6. **Check grammar and spelling.** Read the completed sentence. Does it make sense? Is the word
   copied exactly?

## Worked example (from the passage)

Passage extract:

> "Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, yet they support roughly a quarter
> of all marine species. Their decline is mainly linked to rising sea temperatures, which cause
> corals to expel the algae living in their tissues. This process, known as bleaching, leaves the
> coral white and, if it continues for several weeks, often leads to its death."

Summary (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS):

> Although reefs occupy a tiny area of the seabed, they are home to about a quarter of ocean
> **(1)** ____. The main threat is higher **(2)** ____, which forces corals to lose the
> **(3)** ____ inside them. If this lasts for a number of **(4)** ____, the coral usually dies.

Answers:
1. **species** – "marine species" would repeat "ocean", so "species" alone fits.
2. **sea temperatures** – two words; "temperatures" alone is also acceptable because it fits.
3. **algae** – a plural noun after "the".
4. **weeks** – "several weeks" is paraphrased as "a number of ____".

## Worked example (from a box)

> Bleaching happens when corals are under **(5)** ____ from warmer water.
> A. stress B. protection C. growth D. pressure from fishing

The passage never says "stress", but the meaning of temperatures forcing corals to expel algae
is a form of stress. The answer is **A**. Option D contains the word "pressure", but fishing is
not mentioned. With a word box, always match **meaning**, not words.

## Common traps

- **Breaking the word limit.** "rising sea temperatures" is three words; with a two-word limit
  it is wrong.
- **Changing word forms.** If the passage says "expel", you cannot write "expulsion".
- **Grammar mismatch.** Check articles ("a/an" before the gap), plurals and verb forms.
- **Spelling mistakes when copying.** Copy letter by letter.
- **Choosing a box word because it appears in the passage.** Box options are often paraphrases.

## Practice idea

Take a 200-word section of any article. Write your own five-gap summary of it, using different
words from the original wherever possible, and leave gaps only where a passage word can be
copied. Swap with a study partner, or come back to it two days later and complete it under a
four-minute limit.
