## Three related tasks

These tasks often appear with **technical or scientific passages**: how a machine works, how a
product is made, how an animal's body functions.

- **Diagram labelling:** a picture with numbered labels. You complete each label with words from
  the passage, or choose letters from a list.
- **Flow-chart completion:** boxes and arrows showing a sequence of stages. You fill the gaps.
- **Short-answer questions:** direct questions ("What…?", "Which…?", "How many…?") that you answer
  in a few words.

All three normally use words **from the passage** and have a word limit.

## Why the structure helps you

A diagram or flow chart is a visual summary of one section of the passage. This means:

- The answers are usually found **close together** in one or two paragraphs.
- Flow charts follow the **same order** as the passage description.
- Diagrams give you **position clues**: top, bottom, inside, attached to.

Short-answer questions also follow passage order.

## Step-by-step method for diagrams

1. Look at the diagram and find the parts that are already labelled.
2. Scan the passage for those labelled words. The paragraph describing the diagram will contain
   them.
3. For each gap, use the position and the arrows. If label 5 is at the top and the passage
   describes the object from bottom to top, the answer will come later in the description.
4. Copy the word exactly and check the limit.

## Step-by-step method for flow charts

1. Read the title of the flow chart. It tells you which process to look for.
2. Read every box, including the completed ones. These are anchors.
3. Find the first anchor in the passage, then read forward stage by stage.
4. Look for sequence words in the text: *initially, then, once this is done, after that,
   finally*.
5. Check that each answer fits grammatically in its box.

## Step-by-step method for short answers

1. Underline the question word (what, where, how many) and the key words.
2. Decide the answer type: a number, a material, a name, a place.
3. Scan for the key words and read carefully.
4. Write **only** the answer words. Do not repeat the question.

## Worked example: flow chart

Passage extract:

> "To make traditional paper from mulberry bark, workers first soak the bark in a river for
> several days to soften it. The softened bark is then boiled with wood ash, which breaks down the
> fibres. Once cool, the fibres are beaten with wooden hammers until they form a smooth pulp. This
> pulp is mixed with water in a large vat, and a bamboo screen is dipped into the mixture to
> collect a thin layer. Finally, the sheets are pressed and left to dry in the sun."

Flow chart (ONE WORD ONLY):

> Bark soaked in a **(1)** ____ → Boiled with wood **(2)** ____ → Fibres beaten into **(3)**
> ____ → Thin layer collected with a bamboo **(4)** ____ → Sheets dried in the **(5)** ____

Answers: (1) **river** (2) **ash** (3) **pulp** (4) **screen** (5) **sun**

## Worked example: short answer

> *What tool is used to beat the fibres?* (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS)

Answer: **wooden hammers** (or **hammers**). Writing "they are beaten with wooden hammers" is
wrong because it is far over the limit.

## Common traps

- **Breaking the word limit** in short answers by writing a sentence.
- **Losing the order.** If you find answer 3 before answer 2, check that you have the right
  section.
- **Using your own words.** These tasks almost always require words from the passage.
- **Being frightened by technical vocabulary.** You do not need to understand every word. You
  need to locate and copy the correct one.

## Practice idea

Find a short description of a process on a science or cookery website (how glass is recycled,
how bread is made). Draw a flow chart of the process with six boxes and remove three key words.
Then come back to the text the next day and complete the chart within three minutes. This
trains you to connect the text with its visual structure.
