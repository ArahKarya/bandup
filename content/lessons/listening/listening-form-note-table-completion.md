## Why these tasks matter

Form, note and table completion appear in almost every Listening test, especially in Part 1 and
Part 4. The language is often simple: names, addresses, dates, prices, times. That makes these
questions **the easiest marks to gain and the easiest to lose**. Most lost marks are not about
understanding. They come from spelling, word limits and numbers.

## The three formats

- **Form completion:** an official form, such as a booking or a membership application. Answers
  are usually personal details and facts.
- **Note completion:** short notes with headings and bullet points, common in Part 4 lectures.
- **Table completion:** information organised in rows and columns. Reading across a row and down
  a column helps you predict.

## Word limits

Always read the instruction carefully:

- **ONE WORD ONLY** – a single word, no numbers needed.
- **ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER** – for example "Thursday", "42" or "42 boxes".
- **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER** – up to two words plus a number.

If you write more than the limit, the answer is wrong even if the information is correct.
Hyphenated words ("well-known") count as one word. A number written in figures ("15") counts as
a number, not a word.

## Spelling and numbers

Part 1 often includes a name or street that the speaker **spells out**. Practise the English
alphabet until letters like *A/E/I*, *G/J* and *B/V/P* are automatic. Common confusions for many
learners:

- "E" sounds like the Indonesian "i"; "I" sounds like "ai".
- "double S" means "SS".
- "W" is "double-u".

For numbers, listen for:

- **-teen versus -ty:** "fourteen" stresses the second syllable; "forty" stresses the first.
- **Telephone numbers:** "oh" means zero, and "double five" means 55.
- **Dates:** "the third of May" can be written "3 May" or "May 3"; both are usually accepted.
- **Money:** you do not need to write the currency if the symbol is already on the page.

Common words you must spell correctly include: *accommodation, February, Wednesday, necessary,
library, environment, government, restaurant, separate, beginning*.

## Step-by-step method

1. Read the instruction and circle the word limit.
2. Read the title and headings to understand the situation.
3. For tables, read the column headings and one complete row. The completed rows show what type
   of answer each column needs.
4. Predict the word type for each gap.
5. While listening, write quickly. You can tidy spelling later.
6. In the transfer or check time, check every answer for **spelling, plurals and word limits**.

## Worked example

> **Hire details**
> - Item: ____ (1)
> - Collection time: ____ (2) a.m.
> - Deposit: £____ (3)
> - Postcode: ____ (4)

Recording:

> "I'd like to hire a tent for the weekend… a four-person one. Can I collect it at nine? Oh, no,
> half past eight would be better… The deposit is usually thirty pounds, but for tents it's
> forty… And my postcode is BH7 4RJ."

Answers: (1) **tent**; (2) **8.30** (or 8:30); (3) **40**; (4) **BH7 4RJ**.

Why not "four-person tent"? Because "four-person" is one hyphenated word and "tent" is another,
so the answer has two words. If the instruction allowed only one word, it would be marked wrong.
The key noun "tent" is enough. For (2), note that "nine" was a distractor: the speaker changed
the time to half past eight.

## Common traps

- **Plural forms.** If you hear "brushes", writing "brush" is wrong.
- **Extra words.** Do not write "on Monday" if the gap already follows "on".
- **Distractors.** "Thirty… but for tents it's forty": the speaker changes the information.
- **Guessing from general knowledge.** The answer must come from the recording.
- **Messy handwriting** on paper. If the examiner cannot read it, it is wrong.

## Practice idea

Make a "spelling list" of 30 words that often appear in Part 1: months, days, street words
(avenue, crescent, lane), and words such as *accommodation* and *questionnaire*. Ask a friend or a
text-to-speech tool to read them, write them down, and check. Repeat the words you get wrong
every day for a week.
