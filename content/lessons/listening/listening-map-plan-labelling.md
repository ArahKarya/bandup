## What the task looks like

Map and plan labelling usually appears in **Part 2**. You see a map of a park, a campus, a town
centre or the floor plan of a building. Some places are labelled with letters (A–H). You must
match each place in the questions (for example "13. Visitor centre") to the correct letter.
Sometimes you write a word into a labelled space instead. The speaker is often a guide describing
the area to visitors.

## Language you must know

**Position**

- next to, beside, opposite, diagonally opposite
- between X and Y, in the corner, at the far end
- in front of, behind, at the back of
- to the north / south-east of
- on your left / right (as you face a direction)
- halfway along, at the junction, where the two paths meet

**Movement**

- go straight on / carry on / keep going
- turn left / take the first right / bear right (turn slightly)
- go past, go through, cross over, follow the path round
- double back (return the way you came)
- as you come in, once you are through the gate

**Shapes and features**

- a roundabout, a crossroads, a T-junction, a footbridge, a pond, a car park, a courtyard,
  an entrance, a reception desk, a staircase, a lift

## Step-by-step method

1. **Find the starting point.** Look for "You are here", an entrance, or a gate. If there is
   none, the speaker will tell you at the start.
2. **Find the compass or the direction you face.** If the map shows north at the top, "north"
   means up. Speakers also say "at the top of the map".
3. **Name the fixed features.** Note any already-labelled places: a lake, the main road, a
   statue. These are anchors the speaker will use.
4. **Read the question list.** The questions are in the order you will hear them. Sometimes they
   follow a route; sometimes they jump around.
5. **Follow the route with your pencil** as you listen. Do not just listen; move.
6. **Check left and right** carefully. If the speaker walks "down" the map, their left is your
   right.

## Worked example

Imagine a small park map. The main gate is at the bottom (south). A path goes north from the gate
to a pond in the centre. Letters: **A** is just west of the gate, **B** is east of the path
halfway to the pond, **C** is on the north side of the pond, **D** is in the north-east corner,
**E** is west of the pond.

Recording:

> "As you come in through the main gate, the first thing on your right, about halfway up the
> path, is the children's playground. Keep going until you reach the pond. If you go round to
> the far side, that's where you'll find the café, looking out over the water. People often
> think the toilets are next to the café, but in fact they're up in the top right-hand corner of
> the park."

Answers:
- Playground: **B**. You enter facing north; "on your right" is east, "halfway up the path".
- Café: **C**. "The far side" of the pond from the gate is the north side.
- Toilets: **D**. "Top right-hand corner" is the north-east. Note the distractor: "people often
  think… next to the café".

## Common traps

- **Mixing up your left and the speaker's left.** Turn the page if it helps, or imagine yourself
  walking on the map.
- **Losing the route.** If you miss one place, look for the next fixed landmark and join the
  speaker again.
- **Ignoring corrections.** "It used to be by the lake, but it's moved."
- **Forgetting that letters may be unused.** There are usually more letters than questions.

## Practice idea

Draw a simple map of your street, school or office with six places on it. Record yourself
describing how to get from the entrance to each place, using at least eight phrases from the
lists above. Then listen back and follow your own directions. Doing the speaker's job is the
quickest way to understand their language.
