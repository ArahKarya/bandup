## The structure of the test

The Listening test has **four parts and 40 questions**, and it lasts about 30 minutes. Each
recording is played **only once**. The parts become more difficult as you go:

| Part | Speakers | Context | Typical tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two people | Everyday, e.g. booking a course, renting a flat | Form or note completion |
| 2 | One person | Everyday, e.g. a tour guide, a radio announcement | Multiple choice, map labelling |
| 3 | Two to four people | Education, e.g. students discussing a project with a tutor | Multiple choice, matching |
| 4 | One person | An academic lecture | Note or sentence completion |

Questions follow the order of the recording. If you hear the answer to question 14, the answer to
question 13 has already passed.

## Why prediction works

Before each part you have a short time (around 30 seconds) to read the questions. Strong
listeners do not just read; they **predict**. When you know what kind of information you are
waiting for, your brain can recognise it much faster. Prediction has three levels:

1. **Grammar:** Does the gap need a noun, a verb, an adjective or a number?
2. **Meaning:** Is it a day, a price, a name, a colour, a reason?
3. **Context:** What is the situation? Who is talking and why?

## A step-by-step method

1. **Read the instructions first.** Note the word limit, for example "ONE WORD AND/OR A
   NUMBER".
2. **Look at the title and headings.** They tell you the context.
3. **Underline key words** in each question, especially names, dates and words that are easy to
   recognise.
4. **Predict the answer type** for each gap and write a small note next to it: *n* (noun),
   *num*, *£*, *day*, *pl* (plural noun).
5. **Think of synonyms.** If the question says "cost", the speaker may say "price", "fee" or "you
   pay".
6. **While listening, keep your eyes one question ahead.** If you miss an answer, move on.

## Worked example

Imagine these notes before Part 1:

> **Riverside Pottery Classes**
> - Day of beginners' class: ____
> - Length of each session: ____ hours
> - Bring: an old ____ to protect clothes
> - Total fee: £____

Prediction:
- Gap 1: a day of the week. Expect possible changes: "Monday… actually that's full, so Thursday".
- Gap 2: a number, probably small (1–3).
- Gap 3: a singular noun for clothing protection, perhaps "shirt" or "apron". The word "an"
  suggests it begins with a vowel sound, so "apron" is very likely.
- Gap 4: a price. Listen for discounts or extra costs that change the final figure.

Now the recording:

> "The beginners' group used to meet on Mondays, but we've moved it to Wednesday evenings… Each
> session runs for two and a half hours. Oh, and please bring an old apron, or a big shirt if you
> don't have one. The course is £95, although that includes all the clay."

Answers: **Wednesday**, **2.5**, **apron**, **95**. Notice that "Monday" was mentioned first as a
distractor, and that "shirt" appeared but was not the main item. Because you predicted, you were
ready to wait for the final, correct information.

## Common traps

- **Writing the first thing you hear.** Speakers often correct themselves. Wait until the idea is
  finished.
- **Ignoring grammar clues.** "a ____" needs a singular noun; "several ____" needs a plural.
- **Getting lost.** If two answers pass while you are thinking, look at the next question and
  listen for its key word. One lost answer is better than five.
- **Using preview time to fix old answers.** Fix them in the transfer or check time at the end.

## Practice idea

Take any BandUp Listening Part 1 set. Before you press play, spend two minutes writing a
prediction next to every gap (word type and meaning). After you finish, compare the predictions
with the correct answers. Count how many predictions were right. With practice, you should be
right about the word type at least 80% of the time.
