## What makes Part 4 different

Part 4 is a **lecture or talk by one speaker** on an academic subject: the history of a
material, a study on animal behaviour, a new technology. There are ten questions, usually note
completion, and **there is normally no pause in the middle**. The speaker talks for around four
to five minutes with no interruptions from other people. The vocabulary is more academic, and
the ideas are more abstract than in Parts 1–3.

The good news is that lectures are **highly organised**. Lecturers tell you where they are going,
what they are explaining, and when they are moving on. These phrases are called **signposts**. If
you learn to hear them, you will rarely get lost.

## Key signposting language

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Introducing the topic | Today I'm going to look at…, I'd like to focus on… |
| Ordering points | First of all…, The second factor is…, Finally… |
| Moving on | Turning now to…, Let's move on to…, That brings me to… |
| Giving an example | For instance…, Take the case of…, A good example is… |
| Rephrasing | In other words…, What I mean is…, Put simply… |
| Contrast | However…, On the other hand…, Despite this… |
| Cause and effect | As a result…, This led to…, Because of this… |
| Emphasis | What's crucial here is…, It's worth noting that… |
| Concluding | To sum up…, So, to conclude… |

## Step-by-step method

1. **Read all ten questions in the preview time.** Do not just read the first few.
2. **Use the headings.** Note completion in Part 4 usually has headings such as "Background",
   "Methods", "Findings". Each heading matches a section of the lecture.
3. **Predict the word type** for each gap, as in Part 1. Academic gaps are often nouns: a
   material, a process, a result.
4. **Listen for signposts** between sections. When you hear "Turning now to the results", your
   eyes should jump to the "Findings" heading.
5. **Expect paraphrase.** The note says "became popular because of its low ____". The lecturer
   might say "people chose it mainly since it didn't cost much to produce", so the answer is
   "cost".
6. **Keep moving.** If an answer is missed, the next signpost will help you rejoin.

## Worked example

Notes:

> **The history of bamboo as a building material**
> *Early uses*
> - Used for bridges and ____ (31) in parts of Asia
> *Advantages*
> - Grows very ____ (32)
> - Strength similar to some types of ____ (33)
> *Problems*
> - Can be damaged by ____ (34) unless treated

Lecture:

> "Today I'd like to look at bamboo as a building material. Historically, communities in parts of
> Asia relied on it for bridges and also scaffolding, which is still common in some cities. So
> why bamboo? Well, the most obvious advantage is its speed of growth. Some species grow almost a
> metre a day, so it grows extremely rapidly. And despite its light weight, it's surprisingly
> strong; in fact, its tensile strength has been compared to that of some steels. On the
> downside, however, untreated bamboo is vulnerable to insects, which can destroy a structure
> within a few years."

Answers: (31) **scaffolding**; (32) **rapidly** (an adverb, which fits "grows very ____"); (33) **steel**; (34) **insects**.

Notice how each heading matched a signpost: "Historically" (early uses), "the most obvious
advantage" (advantages), "On the downside, however" (problems).

## Common traps

- **Not reading the whole page.** Because there is no pause, you must preview all ten questions.
- **Writing the example instead of the main point.** After "for instance", the speaker may give a
  detail that is not the answer.
- **Grammar mismatch.** "Grows very ____" needs an adverb, so "rapid" (adjective) is wrong.
- **Spelling academic words.** Practise words like *phenomenon, analysis, hypothesis,
  temperature, vegetation*.

## Practice idea

Watch a short academic talk online (five to ten minutes). Before watching, draw three empty
headings on a page. While listening, write each signpost you hear and the main point after it.
Afterwards, check whether your notes form a clear outline of the talk. If they do, you are
following the lecturer's structure, which is exactly the skill Part 4 tests.
