## How multiple choice is built

In Listening, multiple-choice questions usually have three options (A, B, C), or a longer list
when you must "Choose TWO letters". They appear most often in Parts 2 and 3. The test writer
makes sure that **all three options are mentioned or suggested in the recording**. Only one
matches the speaker's real meaning. The others are **distractors**.

## The main types of distractor

1. **The correction:** a speaker gives information and then changes it. "We meet at the café…
   actually, it's being repaired, so we'll use the library."
2. **The rejected idea:** someone suggests something, and the other person rejects it. "What
   about a survey?" "I think that would take too long."
3. **The word match:** an option uses the same word you hear, but the meaning is different. You
   hear "cheap", and an option says "cheap", but the speaker said it *wasn't* cheap.
4. **The past versus now:** "It used to be a factory" is not the same as "It is a factory".
5. **The general versus specific:** the speaker mentions a topic in general, but the question asks
   for a specific reason or detail.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Read the question stem first** and underline the key idea. "What does Jake say is the
   *main problem* with the survey?"
2. **Paraphrase each option in your head.** For "It was too expensive", think "cost a lot, over
   budget, couldn't afford it".
3. **Listen for the whole idea**, not single words. Wait until a speaker finishes a point.
4. **Cross out options** as they are rejected or contradicted.
5. **Watch for signal words** that introduce the true answer: "in the end", "actually", "the real
   issue is", "what really matters", "so we decided".
6. **Do not stay on one question.** If the next question's key words start, move on.

## Worked example

> **Question:** Why did Maya choose the topic of urban gardens for her project?
> A. Her tutor recommended it.
> B. She had personal experience of it.
> C. There was a lot of published research.

Recording:

> **Tutor:** So, Maya, why urban gardens? I remember suggesting transport.
> **Maya:** Yes, you did, and I thought about it. And I was hoping there'd be plenty of studies
> on gardens, but actually there isn't much. Really, it's because I've been volunteering at a
> community garden near my flat for two years, so I know the problems first-hand.

Analysis:
- **A** is a distractor. The tutor suggested a *different* topic.
- **C** is a distractor. She expected lots of research, but "there isn't much".
- **B** is correct. "Volunteering… for two years… first-hand" paraphrases "personal
  experience". Notice the signal "Really, it's because".

## Choose TWO letters

When you must choose two answers from five, the recording may discuss all five options. Make a
small tick or cross next to each one as you listen. Usually the speakers agree on two, reject two
and are unsure about one. Write both letters; the order does not matter, and each letter is
worth one mark.

## Common traps

- **Answering too early.** The first option mentioned is often a distractor.
- **Choosing based on your opinion.** The answer is what the speaker thinks, not what you
  think.
- **Missing negatives.** "I wouldn't say it was difficult" means it was *not* difficult.
- **Confusing speakers.** In Part 3, note who holds which opinion. The question may ask about
  the tutor, not the student.

## Practice idea

After completing a BandUp Part 3 set, open the script. For each question, highlight where each
of the three options is mentioned and label it "correct", "corrected", "rejected" or "word
match". Doing this five or six times trains you to recognise distractor patterns during the real
test.
