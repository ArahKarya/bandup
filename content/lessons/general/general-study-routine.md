## Why routine matters more than motivation

Most IELTS candidates start with lots of energy, study for six hours on Saturday, and then do
nothing until the next weekend. Progress in a language comes from **frequent, focused practice**,
not from occasional heroic sessions. A routine you can keep for two months will raise your band
more than a perfect plan you abandon after ten days.

## Step 1: Measure before you plan

Before you decide what to study, find out where you are. Take one timed Listening set, one
Reading set, write one Task 2 essay, and record yourself answering a Speaking Part 2 card. Write
down an estimated band for each. Compare these with your target. The section with the **biggest
gap** gets the most time.

Be honest. If you cannot judge your Writing or Speaking, ask a teacher or use BandUp's feedback
tools, and look carefully at the band descriptors.

## Step 2: Choose a realistic weekly shape

A good weekly routine has three ingredients:

1. **Skill practice** – short, focused work on one question type or one writing task.
2. **Language building** – vocabulary and grammar that you will need in every section.
3. **Review** – going back over mistakes.

Here is an example for someone studying about 90 minutes a day, five days a week, whose weakest
section is Writing:

| Day | Main focus (60 min) | Daily extra (30 min) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Writing Task 2: plan and write one essay | 15 new vocabulary cards + review |
| Tuesday | Reading: one passage, then analyse mistakes | Listening to a podcast, noting phrases |
| Wednesday | Writing Task 1: one report, then compare with a model | Vocabulary review |
| Thursday | Listening: one part, check script for missed answers | Speaking Part 1 recording |
| Friday | Rewrite Monday's essay using feedback | Speaking Part 2 recording |

On the weekend, rest or do one longer activity such as a full mock test every second week.

## Step 3: Mix input and output

**Input** means reading and listening. **Output** means speaking and writing. Many learners do
only input because it feels comfortable. But the Writing and Speaking bands depend on your
ability to *produce* language quickly. Make sure that at least one third of your weekly time is
output. When you meet a useful phrase in a reading passage, use it in your next essay. This
connects the two halves of your study.

## Step 4: Keep an error log

The single habit that separates fast improvers from slow ones is an **error log**. After every
practice task, write down each mistake in a simple table:

| Date | Section | Question type | What I wrote | Correct answer | Why I was wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Nov | Reading | TFNG | FALSE | NOT GIVEN | The passage never mentioned price; I used my own knowledge |
| 4 Nov | Listening | Form completion | Febuary | February | Spelling |

After two weeks, read the log and look for **patterns**. Perhaps half your Reading errors are
NOT GIVEN questions, or most Listening errors are spelling. These patterns tell you exactly
which BandUp lesson to study next. BandUp's weakness heatmap does the same thing automatically
for practice questions, but your own notes add the "why".

## Worked example: an 8-week plan

Andi needs 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0. His starting estimates are Listening 6.5, Reading
6.0, Writing 5.5, Speaking 6.0.

- **Weeks 1–2:** Learn the format of every section. Study the Writing criteria lesson and the
  Task 2 planning lesson. Two essays per week. Vocabulary every day.
- **Weeks 3–5:** Focus on weaknesses. Three writing tasks per week with rewriting. Reading
  practice on the question types his log shows are weak. Speaking recordings three times a week.
- **Weeks 6–7:** One full mock test each weekend under real timing. Review every mistake.
- **Week 8:** Lighter work. Revisit the error log, rest well, and do one final mock early in the
  week, not the day before the test.

## Common traps

- **Doing test after test without review.** A test only shows your level. The learning happens
  when you analyse the mistakes.
- **Studying only what you enjoy.** If you love Listening, it is tempting to do it every day. Your
  overall band moves faster when the weakest section improves.
- **Learning word lists without context.** Learn each new word with a collocation and an example
  sentence, then use it within a few days.
- **No rest.** Tired brains learn badly. One day off per week is part of the plan, not a failure.

## Practice idea

Draw your own weekly table for the next seven days. Put your weakest section on at least three
days, add 15 minutes of vocabulary to each day, and create an empty error log. At the end of the
week, count how many sessions you actually completed. If it is fewer than you planned, make the
plan smaller rather than giving up. A smaller plan you keep beats a bigger one you break.
