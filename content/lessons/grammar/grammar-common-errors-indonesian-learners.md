## Why these errors happen

Bahasa Indonesia and English work very differently in some areas of grammar. Indonesian has **no
articles** like *a* and *the*, **no required plural endings** (plurals are often shown by
repetition, *buku-buku*, or by context), **no verb changes for the subject**, and **no verb
changes for time** (time is shown with words like *sudah*, *akan*, *kemarin*). These four
differences cause the most frequent errors in IELTS Writing and Speaking for Indonesian
learners. The good news is that because the errors are predictable, you can check for them
systematically.

## 1. Articles (a, an, the)

**Basic rules**

- **a / an** with a **singular countable** noun mentioned for the first time or meaning "one of
  many": *I bought **a** laptop.*
- **the** when both the writer and reader know which one: ***The** laptop I bought is fast.*
- **the** with unique things: *the sun, the internet, the government* (of a country), *the
  environment*.
- **No article** with plural or uncountable nouns used in a general sense: ***Children** need
  **education**.* (not *The children need the education*)

**Typical errors**

- ✗ *She is teacher.* → ✓ *She is **a** teacher.*
- ✗ *The technology changes our lives.* → ✓ *Technology changes our lives.*
- ✗ *Government should reduce taxes.* → ✓ ***The** government should reduce taxes.*

**Tip:** every singular countable noun needs something in front of it: *a, an, the, my, this,
one, every*. If there is nothing, check.

## 2. Plurals

After numbers and quantity words, countable nouns must be plural.

- ✗ *There are three reason.* → ✓ *There are three **reasons**.*
- ✗ *Many student prefer online classes.* → ✓ *Many **students**…*
- ✗ *one of the best city* → ✓ *one of the best **cities***

Some nouns are **uncountable** and have no plural: *information, advice, equipment, research,
furniture, homework, traffic, knowledge*.

- ✗ *informations, advices, researches* → ✓ *information, pieces of advice, research*

## 3. Subject-verb agreement

In the present simple, **he / she / it / a singular noun** takes **-s**.

- ✗ *My father work in Medan.* → ✓ *My father **works** in Medan.*
- ✗ *Technology make life easier.* → ✓ *Technology **makes** life easier.*

Tricky cases:

- **Long subjects:** *The **number** of cars on the roads **has** increased.* The verb agrees
  with "number", not "roads".
- **Each / every / everyone:** *Every student **has** a laptop.*
- **Uncountable nouns:** *Information **is** easy to find online.*
- **"There is / there are"** agrees with the noun after it: *There **are** many reasons.*

## 4. Tense

English shows time in the verb. Choose the tense based on **when** and **how** something happens.

| Situation | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Habits and facts | Present simple | *I **take** the train every day.* |
| Finished past time | Past simple | *I **moved** to Bali in 2019.* |
| Past to now / experience | Present perfect | *I **have lived** here for five years.* |
| Future plan | going to / present continuous | *I**'m going to** study in Australia.* |

**Typical errors**

- ✗ *Last year I visit my grandparents.* → ✓ *Last year I **visited**…*
- ✗ *I live here since 2018.* → ✓ *I **have lived** here since 2018.*
- ✗ *I have finished university in 2020.* → ✓ *I **finished** university in 2020.* (a finished
  time needs the past simple)

In Speaking, many candidates use the present tense for past stories in Part 2. Decide before you
start: "This happened in the past, so I will use past forms."

## Worked example: editing a paragraph

Original:

> *In my city, government build many new road last year. Now the traffic become better, but
> pollution is still big problem. My friend say that the people should use the public transport
> more.*

Corrected:

> *In my city, **the** government **built** many new **roads** last year. Now **the** traffic
> **has become** better, but pollution is still **a** big problem. My friend **says** that
> people should use public transport more.*

Eight changes: two articles added ("the government", "a big problem"), one plural ("roads"),
two tenses ("built", "has become"), one agreement ("says"), and two articles removed ("the
people", "the public transport", which are general). "The traffic" stays, because it means the
traffic in this specific city.

## Common traps

- Fixing only the errors you notice in Writing but not in Speaking. Practise both.
- Overcorrecting by adding *the* everywhere. Remember: general plural and uncountable nouns
  usually take no article.
- Adding -s to uncountable nouns: *equipments, knowledges*.

## Practice idea

Create a four-point checklist: **Article? Plural? -s? Tense?** After each piece of writing, read
it four times, once for each point. Track how many corrections you make in your error log. Most
learners see these errors drop by half within a month of checking every time.
