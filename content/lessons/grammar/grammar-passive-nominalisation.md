## Why academic style matters

In IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 and in Task 2 essays, a more **objective and formal** style helps
your Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range scores. Two tools make writing sound academic:

1. The **passive voice**, which focuses on actions and results instead of people.
2. **Nominalisation**, which turns verbs and adjectives into nouns so that ideas become more
   compact and abstract.

Both should be used **with purpose**, not in every sentence.

## Part 1: The passive voice

### Form

**be** (in the correct tense) + **past participle**

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | They recycle the glass. | The glass **is recycled**. |
| Past simple | They opened the museum in 2001. | The museum **was opened** in 2001. |
| Present perfect | They have closed the factory. | The factory **has been closed**. |
| Future | They will build a new airport. | A new airport **will be built**. |
| Modal | We should reduce emissions. | Emissions **should be reduced**. |

### When to use it

- The doer is **unknown, obvious or unimportant**: *The data **was collected** in 2023.*
- You are describing a **process** (Task 1): *The beans **are roasted** and then **ground**.*
- You want to sound **objective**: *It **is often argued** that…* instead of *I think people
  say…*
- You want to keep the **topic at the start** of the sentence: *Plastic bags were banned in
  2020. **The ban was introduced** after a public campaign.*

### Impersonal passives

These are very useful in Task 2:

- ***It is widely believed** that education reduces inequality.*
- ***It has been suggested** that working hours should be reduced.*
- *Remote work **is thought to** improve productivity.*

## Part 2: Nominalisation

Nominalisation means expressing an idea as a **noun phrase** rather than a verb or adjective
clause.

| Verb / adjective | Noun |
|---|---|
| develop | development |
| fail | failure |
| decide | decision |
| grow | growth |
| important | importance |
| available | availability |
| able | ability |
| reduce | reduction |

### Why it helps

- **Compact ideas:** *Because the population grew rapidly, schools became overcrowded.* →
  ***The rapid growth of the population** led to overcrowded schools.*
- **Better cohesion:** a noun can refer back to a whole idea. *Many factories closed. **These
  closures** caused unemployment.*
- **More academic tone:** abstract nouns are typical of formal writing.

### How to nominalise

1. Find the main verb or adjective: *Prices **fell** sharply.*
2. Change it to a noun: *fell → a fall*.
3. Change adverbs to adjectives: *sharply → sharp*.
4. Connect the subject with a preposition: *a sharp fall **in** prices*.
5. Build a new sentence around the noun phrase: ***A sharp fall in prices** was recorded in
   2019.*

## Worked example

Informal version:

> *People are using cars more and more, so the air in cities is getting dirtier. If the
> government taxed fuel, people might drive less.*

Academic version:

> *The **increasing use** of private cars **has led to** a **deterioration** in urban air
> quality. **It has been suggested** that the **introduction** of a fuel tax **would encourage**
> a **reduction** in car journeys.*

Nominalisations: *increasing use, deterioration, introduction, reduction*. Passive: *It has been
suggested*. The second version is shorter in clauses but richer in vocabulary.

## Common traps

- **Overuse.** A paragraph made entirely of nominalised phrases becomes heavy and hard to read.
  Mix with active verbs.
- **Wrong participles:** *was build* → *was built*; *has been choose* → *has been chosen*.
- **Missing "be":** *The factory closed by the government* → *The factory **was** closed…*
- **Wrong prepositions after nouns:** *an increase **in**, a reduction **in**, the importance
  **of**, an effect **on**, the cause **of***.
- **Intransitive verbs cannot be passive:** *The accident was happened* is wrong; *happen* has no
  object.

## Practice idea

Take five sentences from your last essay that start with "people" or "I think". Rewrite each one
using either an impersonal passive (*It is often argued that…*) or a nominalisation (*The growth
of…*). Then read both versions aloud and decide where the academic version is clearer and where
the original is better. Good writers use both.
