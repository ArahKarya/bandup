## What band 7 asks for

The band 7 descriptor for Grammatical Range and Accuracy (Writing) says the candidate:

- uses a **variety of complex structures**;
- produces **frequent error-free sentences**;
- has **good control** of grammar and punctuation, though may make a few errors.

At band 6, candidates use a **mix of simple and complex forms**, but with less flexibility. The
step from 6 to 7 is not about very long sentences. It is about using **different types** of
complex sentence **accurately**.

## Simple, compound and complex

- **Simple:** one clause. *Public transport is cheap.*
- **Compound:** two main clauses joined by *and, but, or, so*. *Public transport is cheap, but it
  is often crowded.*
- **Complex:** a main clause plus a **subordinate clause**. *Although public transport is cheap,
  many people still prefer to drive.*

## Five structures to master

### 1. Relative clauses

**Defining** (no commas) identifies which one: *Students **who work part-time** often have less
time for revision.*

**Non-defining** (commas) adds extra information: *Jakarta, **which has a population of over ten
million**, faces serious traffic problems.*

Rules: use *who* for people, *which* for things; *that* only in defining clauses; never use
*that* after a comma.

### 2. Adverbial (subordinate) clauses

| Meaning | Conjunctions |
|---|---|
| Contrast | although, even though, while, whereas |
| Reason | because, since, as |
| Time | when, once, until, as soon as, before, after |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, as long as |
| Purpose | so that, in order that |

*Unless governments act quickly, many coastal cities will face regular flooding.*

### 3. Conditionals

- **First** (real future): *If cities **build** more cycle lanes, more people **will cycle**.*
- **Second** (hypothetical): *If public transport **were** free, traffic **would decrease**.*
- **Third** (past, unreal): *If the city **had invested** earlier, the floods **would have caused**
  less damage.*
- **Mixed:** *If I **had studied** harder at school, I **would be** more confident now.*

### 4. Participle clauses

These make writing more concise and academic.

- **Present participle (-ing), active:** *Living in a big city, young people have access to many
  opportunities.* (= Because they live…)
- **Past participle, passive:** *Introduced in 2015, the policy has reduced plastic waste.*
  (= The policy, which was introduced…)
- **After a conjunction:** *After finishing university, many graduates move abroad.*

The subject of the participle must be the same as the subject of the main clause. *Walking to
work, the rain started* is wrong: the rain was not walking.

### 5. Noun clauses

- ***What** concerns many parents is the amount of time children spend online.*
- *It is clear **that** further research is needed.*
- *Experts disagree about **whether** the policy will work.*

## Worked example: upgrading a paragraph

Band 5–6 version:

> *Many people move to cities. They want better jobs. Cities become crowded. The government
> should build more houses. Then prices will go down.*

Band 7 version:

> *Attracted by better job prospects, many people move from rural areas to cities, which
> inevitably become overcrowded. If governments invested in affordable housing, prices would
> stabilise, and what is now a crisis could become a manageable challenge.*

Structures used: past participle clause ("Attracted by…"), non-defining relative clause
("which inevitably become…"), second conditional, noun clause ("what is now a crisis").

## Common traps

- **Comma splices:** *Prices rose, people complained.* Use a conjunction or a full stop.
- **Fragments:** *Because the city is crowded.* A subordinate clause cannot stand alone.
- **Dangling participles:** the subject of the -ing phrase must match the main subject.
- **Wrong conditional forms:** *If I would have more time…* should be *If I had more time…*.
- **Complexity at the cost of accuracy.** A clear simple sentence is better than a confusing
  long one.

## Practice idea

Write five simple sentences about your city. Then rewrite each one as a complex sentence, using a
different structure each time: a relative clause, an adverbial clause, a conditional, a
participle clause and a noun clause. Check every sentence for commas and verb forms. Then try the
BandUp grammar practice sets to test your accuracy.
