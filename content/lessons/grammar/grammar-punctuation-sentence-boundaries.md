## Why punctuation affects your band

Grammatical Range and Accuracy includes **punctuation**. The band 7 descriptor mentions "good
control of grammar and punctuation", and band 6 says errors in grammar and punctuation "rarely
reduce communication". More importantly, punctuation errors often make your complex sentences
**incorrect**. A sentence that could show band 7 range becomes an error if its boundaries are
wrong.

## Three boundary errors

### 1. Comma splice

Two main clauses joined only by a comma.

- ✗ *Online learning is flexible, it can be lonely.*

Fix it in one of four ways:

- **Full stop:** *Online learning is flexible. It can be lonely.*
- **Conjunction:** *Online learning is flexible, **but** it can be lonely.*
- **Semicolon:** *Online learning is flexible; it can, however, be lonely.*
- **Subordination:** ***Although** online learning is flexible, it can be lonely.*

### 2. Run-on sentence

Two or more main clauses with no punctuation at all.

- ✗ *Many people work from home this saves time it also reduces traffic.*
- ✓ *Many people work from home. This saves time and also reduces traffic.*

### 3. Fragment

An incomplete sentence: usually a subordinate clause or a phrase standing alone.

- ✗ *Because public transport is unreliable.*
- ✗ *For example, the new metro line in Jakarta.*
- ✓ *Many people drive because public transport is unreliable.*
- ✓ *For example, the new metro line in Jakarta has reduced journey times.*

Be careful with *Because*, *Although*, *Which* and *For example* at the start of a sentence. Check
that a main clause follows.

## Commas: the main rules

1. **After an introductory clause or phrase:** *If the tax is introduced, prices will rise.* /
   *In 2010, the figure stood at 20%.*
2. **After linking adverbs at the start:** *However, … / Therefore, … / In addition, …*
3. **Before a conjunction joining two main clauses** (and, but, so, yet): *Sales rose, but
   profits fell.*
4. **Around non-defining relative clauses:** *Bali, which attracts millions of tourists, depends
   on tourism.*
5. **In lists:** *cars, buses, trains and bicycles*.

**No comma** between subject and verb: ✗ *The main reason for this trend, is cost.*

**No comma** before a defining relative clause: ✗ *People, who exercise, live longer.* (This
would mean all people exercise.)

**No comma** before *that* in a noun clause: ✗ *It is clear, that…*

## Semicolons and colons

- **Semicolon (;)** joins two closely related main clauses: *Some cities have invested heavily
  in cycling; others have done almost nothing.*
- **Colon (:)** introduces a list or an explanation: *The plan has one major weakness: cost.*

You do not need these to reach band 7, but using them correctly once or twice shows control.

## However vs although

- ***However*** is an adverb. It connects two sentences: *Prices rose. **However,** demand stayed
  high.* Or use a semicolon: *Prices rose; **however,** demand stayed high.*
- ***Although*** is a conjunction. It joins two clauses in one sentence: ***Although** prices
  rose, demand stayed high.*
- ✗ *Prices rose, however demand stayed high.* This is a comma splice.

## Capital letters

Use capitals for: the start of a sentence, names of people and places, nationalities and
languages (*Indonesian, English*), days and months (*Monday, March*), and the pronoun *I*. Do
**not** use capitals for seasons (*summer*), subjects (*mathematics*, unless a language), or
general nouns in the middle of sentences (*the Government* → *the government*, unless it is part
of an official name).

## Worked example: editing

Original:

> *Nowadays many people live in cities, this cause problems such as traffic and pollution.
> Which make life stressful. However the Government has started a new Project, that encourages
> people to cycle.*

Corrected:

> *Nowadays, many people live in cities, **which causes** problems such as traffic and
> pollution. **These problems** make life stressful. However, the government has started a new
> project **that** encourages people to cycle.*

Fixes: comma after "Nowadays"; comma splice changed to a relative clause (and "cause" corrected
to "causes"); fragment "Which make…" made into a full sentence; comma after "However";
unnecessary capitals removed; comma before defining "that" removed.

## Practice idea

Take your last Task 2 essay and underline every comma. For each one, name the rule it follows
from the list above. If you cannot name a rule, check whether it is a comma splice or an
unnecessary comma. Then find every sentence starting with *Because*, *Which* or *Although* and
make sure it has a main clause.
